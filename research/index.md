# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-microscope" %}Research

Our research focuses on understanding bacterial pathogens, antimicrobial resistance, pathogen evolution and vaccine effectiveness, using an integrated approach that combines clinical microbiology, whole-genome sequencing, genomics, immunology, bioinformatics and translational research.

Our major research areas include:

- Bacterial genomics and genomic surveillance – understanding pathogen diversity, transmission, evolution and clinically important genetic determinants using whole-genome sequencing.
- Antimicrobial resistance and precision therapeutics – characterising resistance mechanisms and translating genomic and phenotypic data into improved antimicrobial treatment strategies, including PK/PD-guided therapy and combination approaches.
- Vaccine immunology and genomic escape – studying functional immunity, antigenic diversity and the evolution of vaccine-relevant pathogens to understand vaccine coverage and potential immune escape.
- Rapid molecular diagnostics and clinical translation – developing and evaluating rapid molecular and sequencing-based approaches for pathogen detection, AMR prediction and clinical decision support.
- Pathogen repositories, reference materials and quality assurance – building well-characterised microbial collections, reference panels and quality-assured laboratory and genomic workflows to support research, surveillance and technology transfer.

Through multicentre and national networks, we aim to bridge laboratory discovery with clinical and public-health applications, generating evidence that can support AMR surveillance, antimicrobial stewardship, vaccine policy, diagnostic development and precision infectious-disease management.
